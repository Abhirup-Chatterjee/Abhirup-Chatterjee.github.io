Talk notes: L-functions over function fields

IMSc, July 2025talk notesfunction fields≈ 8 min read

These are notes from a talk I gave at IMSc in July 2025. The talk followed the framework of Rosen's Number Theory in Function Fields and a paper of J. L. Thunder, and the aim was the same as here: to see why Dirichlet $L$-functions over $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$ are polynomials, what the Riemann hypothesis says about them, and how one computes their average values over a family.

Characters and L-functions

Fix a monic modulus $m \in \mathbb{F}_q[t]$ of degree $M \ge 1$. A Dirichlet character modulo $m$ is a homomorphism $\chi : (\mathbb{F}_q[t]/m)^\times \to \mathbb{C}^\times$, extended by $\chi(f) = 0$ when $\gcd(f, m) \ne 1$. Its $L$-function is $$L(s, \chi) = \sum_{f \text{ monic}} \frac{\chi(f)}{|f|^s} = \prod_{P} \left(1 - \frac{\chi(P)}{|P|^s}\right)^{-1}, \qquad |f| = q^{\deg f}.$$ Writing $u = q^{-s}$ and grouping by degree, $L(u, \chi) = \sum_{n \ge 0} c_n(\chi)\,u^n$ with $c_n(\chi) = \sum_{\deg f = n,\ f \text{ monic}} \chi(f)$.

Full proof: for nontrivial χ, L(u, χ) is a polynomial of degree less than deg m

Proof. Let $n \ge M$. Every monic $f$ of degree $n$ can be written uniquely as $f = g\,m + r$ with $g$ monic of degree $n - M$ and $\deg r < M$; as $r$ runs over all $q^M$ polynomials of degree less than $M$, $f$ runs over a complete set of residues modulo $m$, for each fixed $g$. So $$c_n(\chi) = \sum_{g} \sum_{r \bmod m} \chi(r) = q^{\,n-M}\sum_{r \bmod m}\chi(r) = 0,$$ because the sum of a nontrivial character over the group $(\mathbb{F}_q[t]/m)^\times$ vanishes (multiply by $\chi(a) \ne 1$ for some unit $a$; the sum is unchanged, so it is $0$), and non-units contribute $0$. Hence $c_n(\chi) = 0$ for $n \ge M$. $\square$

So $L(u, \chi)$ has at most $M - 1$ zeros, and we can write $L(u, \chi) = \prod_{j}(1 - \alpha_j u)$. For a primitive character Weil's theorem gives $|\alpha_j| = \sqrt q$, apart from a trivial zero at $u = 1$ when $\chi$ is "even" (trivial on the constants $\mathbb{F}_q^\times$). This is the generalised Riemann hypothesis, proved. Its first consequence is square-root cancellation in character sums over primes, and hence the prime polynomial theorem in arithmetic progressions with error $O(q^{n/2})$; see the post on the Riemann hypothesis for curves.

Mean values in families

With the Riemann hypothesis available, the interesting questions are statistical. Take the family of quadratic characters $\chi_D(f) = \big(\tfrac{D}{f}\big)$ for monic squarefree $D$ of degree $2g + 1$. Then $L(u, \chi_D)$ is the numerator $L_C(u)$ of the zeta function of the hyperelliptic curve $C_D : y^2 = D(x)$, of genus $g$, and its central value $L(\tfrac12, \chi_D)$ is $L_C(q^{-1/2})$. The first moment over the family was computed by Andrade and Keating (2012): $$\sum_{\substack{D \text{ monic squarefree} \\ \deg D = 2g+1}} L\big(\tfrac12, \chi_D\big) = |D|\,\mathcal{P}(g) + O\big(|D|^{3/4 + \varepsilon}\big), \qquad |D| = q^{2g+1},$$ with $\mathcal{P}$ an explicit linear polynomial, the exact analogue of Jutila's theorem over $\mathbb{Q}$. Florea later improved the error term and computed the second, third and fourth moments, where the function-field setting reveals secondary terms that are invisible over the integers. All these results start from an approximate functional equation, which over $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$ is an exact identity because $L$ is a polynomial, and a Poisson summation formula over $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$.

What the talk tried to convey

Three things. First, finiteness: $L$-functions are polynomials, so analytic difficulties (convergence, contour shifting) become algebra. Second, the Riemann hypothesis is a theorem, but it does not make moment problems trivial: the hard part is uniformity in the degree. Third, the random matrix heuristics of Katz and Sarnak predict the leading constants, and in the large-$q$ limit they can be proved; the zeros on the unit circle lab shows the symplectic statistics of this family.

Further reading

  • J. C. Andrade and J. P. Keating, The mean value of $L(\tfrac12, \chi)$ in the hyperelliptic ensemble, Journal of Number Theory 132 (2012).
  • A. Florea, The fourth moment of quadratic Dirichlet L-functions over function fields, Geometric and Functional Analysis 27 (2017).
  • N. M. Katz and P. Sarnak, Random Matrices, Frobenius Eigenvalues, and Monodromy, AMS Colloquium Publications 45, 1999.
