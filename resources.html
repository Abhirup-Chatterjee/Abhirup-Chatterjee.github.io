Resources

Things I find useful

Books, papers, websites and software I return to, including everything cited in the notes, labs and posts on this site, plus my own notes and slides. Aimed at students starting out in number theory.

§Function fields

  • Number Theory in Function Fields Michael Rosen

    The standard introduction to arithmetic in $\mathbb{F}_q[t]$ and beyond. Where I started. On this site: Riemann hypothesis for curves, Talk notes: L-functions over function fields, Zeros on the unit circle, The Möbius Function over Polynomials

  • Algebraic Function Fields and Codes Henning Stichtenoth

    Function fields from the valuation-theoretic side, with the Riemann hypothesis for curves.

  • Random Matrices, Frobenius Eigenvalues, and Monodromy N. M. Katz & P. Sarnak

    Why zeros of L-functions over finite fields follow random matrix statistics. On this site: Zeros on the unit circle

  • Equations over Finite Fields: An Elementary Approach Wolfgang M. Schmidt

    The Stepanov–Bombieri method: the Riemann hypothesis for curves without algebraic geometry. On this site: Riemann hypothesis for curves

§Analytic number theory

  • Multiplicative Number Theory I: Classical Theory H. L. Montgomery & R. C. Vaughan

    Clear and complete; excellent exercises. On this site: Primes from the zeros of ζ

  • Analytic Number Theory H. Iwaniec & E. Kowalski

    The reference for sieve methods, exponential sums and L-functions. On this site: Kloosterman paths and curlicues

  • Multiplicative Number Theory Harold Davenport

    Compact classic on primes in progressions and L-functions. On this site: Primes from the zeros of ζ

  • Introduction to Analytic and Probabilistic Number Theory Gérald Tenenbaum

    Sieves, mean values of arithmetic functions and the probabilistic viewpoint. On this site: Erdős–Kac Lab

  • Statistical Independence in Probability, Analysis and Number Theory Mark Kac

    A short, delightful book on why number theory looks random. On this site: Erdős–Kac Lab

  • Prime Numbers and the Riemann Hypothesis B. Mazur & W. Stein

    The explicit formula with pictures, for a wide audience. On this site: Primes from the zeros of ζ

  • Prime Numbers: A Computational Perspective R. Crandall & C. Pomerance

    Primality testing and factoring algorithms, from trial division to the number field sieve. On this site: Pollard's rho

§Modular forms, elliptic curves & quadratic forms

  • A First Course in Modular Forms F. Diamond & J. Shurman

    A friendly route to modular forms and their L-functions. On this site: The modular surface: j(τ) and CM points

  • A Course in Arithmetic Jean-Pierre Serre

    Quadratic forms, $p$-adic numbers and modular forms in 115 perfect pages. On this site: The modular surface: j(τ) and CM points, The p-adic integers as a fractal

  • Modular Functions and Dirichlet Series in Number Theory Tom M. Apostol

    The eta function, Dedekind sums and Rademacher's partition formula. On this site: Dedekind sums and a fractal scatter

  • Primes of the Form $x^2 + ny^2$ David A. Cox

    Quadratic forms, class field theory and complex multiplication, starting from one question. On this site: The modular surface: j(τ) and CM points, 163 and the class number one problem, Conway's topograph

  • The Arithmetic of Elliptic Curves Joseph H. Silverman

    The graduate standard: isogenies, curves over finite and local fields, Mordell–Weil. On this site: Chord and tangent over 𝔽ₚ, Supersingular isogeny graphs

  • Rational Points on Elliptic Curves J. H. Silverman & J. Tate

    An undergraduate introduction, with the group law done by hand. On this site: Chord and tangent over 𝔽ₚ

  • Elliptic Curves: Number Theory and Cryptography Lawrence C. Washington

    Algorithms and cryptography alongside the theory. On this site: Chord and tangent over 𝔽ₚ

  • The Sensual (Quadratic) Form John H. Conway

    Topographs, rivers and the classification of quadratic forms, in Conway's own style. On this site: Conway's topograph

  • Topology of Numbers Allen Hatcher

    Farey diagrams, continued fractions and Conway's topograph, with hundreds of pictures. On this site: Conway's topograph

  • The Theory of Partitions George E. Andrews

    Generating functions, Rogers–Ramanujan identities and congruences. On this site: Ramanujan's congruences and Dyson's crank

  • Dedekind Sums H. Rademacher & E. Grosswald

    Everything about Dedekind sums, from reciprocity to lattice points. On this site: Dedekind sums and a fractal scatter

§p-adic numbers, continued fractions & approximation

  • p-adic Numbers, p-adic Analysis, and Zeta-Functions Neal Koblitz

    A short, readable entry to $p$-adic analysis. On this site: The p-adic integers as a fractal

  • p-adic Numbers: An Introduction Fernando Q. Gouvêa

    A gentle first course, with Hensel's lemma and $p$-adic analysis. On this site: The p-adic integers as a fractal

  • Continued Fractions A. Ya. Khinchin

    The classic little book: Gauss–Kuzmin, Khinchin's constant and metric theory. On this site: Continued Fraction Digits

  • Ergodic Theory with a view towards Number Theory M. Einsiedler & T. Ward

    The Gauss map, equidistribution and the road to Furstenberg's proof of Szemerédi's theorem. On this site: Continued Fraction Digits

  • Markov's Theorem and 100 Years of the Uniqueness Conjecture Martin Aigner

    Markov numbers from every angle: approximation, trees, words and geometry. On this site: Markov numbers and the uniqueness conjecture

  • The Markoff and Lagrange Spectra T. W. Cusick & M. E. Flahive

    The research monograph on badly approximable numbers. On this site: Markov numbers and the uniqueness conjecture

§Problems, puzzles & proofs

  • Proofs from THE BOOK M. Aigner & G. M. Ziegler

    Erdős's idea of the most elegant proofs, collected. Includes Zagier's one-sentence proof and the Calkin–Wilf tree. On this site: Every fraction exactly once, Zagier's Windmills

  • Unsolved Problems in Number Theory Richard K. Guy

    Hundreds of open problems, from aliquot sequences to Egyptian fractions. On this site: Aliquot sequences, and the mystery of 276

  • Computing the Continuous Discretely M. Beck & S. Robins

    Lattice points in polytopes, Ehrhart theory and Dedekind sums. On this site: Dedekind sums and a fractal scatter

  • Winning Ways for Your Mathematical Plays Berlekamp, Conway & Guy

    Combinatorial game theory, playfully.

§Topology, games & set theory

  • Measure and Category John C. Oxtoby

    A short classic on the duality between measure and Baire category, with the Banach–Mazur game. On this site: Banach–Mazur games

  • Classical Descriptive Set Theory Alexander S. Kechris

    Polish spaces, Choquet games and determinacy: the standard reference. On this site: Banach–Mazur games

  • The Scottish Book R. D. Mauldin (ed.)

    The problems written down at the Scottish Café in Lwów, with commentary. Problem 43 is the Banach–Mazur game. On this site: Banach–Mazur games

§Origami & geometry

  • Origami Design Secrets Robert J. Lang

    Mathematical methods behind modern origami design.

  • Origametry Thomas C. Hull

    The mathematics of paper folding: constructions, axioms and flat-foldability. On this site: A regular pentagon from a strip of paper, Haga's Theorem and Möbius Maps

  • Origamics: Mathematical Explorations Through Paper Folding Kazuo Haga

    Haga's theorems and other folding experiments, aimed at students. On this site: A regular pentagon from a strip of paper, Haga's Theorem and Möbius Maps

  • Projective Geometry H. S. M. Coxeter

    The synthetic classic: Desargues, Pappus and conics. On this site: Shadow Projection

  • Perspectives on Projective Geometry Jürgen Richter-Gebert

    A modern, computational and beautifully illustrated treatment. On this site: Shadow Projection
